PROLOGUESEPTEMBER 19–24

Before the road,
there was New York.

My American journey began before the road trip itself. I arrived in New York on September 19 and checked into the Hilton Garden Inn on Whitehall Street, in Lower Manhattan, close to South Ferry. With several evenings before the drive south and west, I decided to explore the city one neighbourhood at a time.

I reached the hotel before the 3 p.m. check-in time. Rather than wait around after the long flight from Mumbai, I walked to the nearby Staten Island Ferry terminal and boarded a ferry to Staten Island. From the water, I saw the Statue of Liberty — a fitting first landmark, and the first of many monuments and cultural icons I would encounter across the country.

After getting off the ferry, I had lunch at River Dock Cafe, a picturesque bistro overlooking the Hudson River. That evening, I took the subway for the first time and headed to Times Square. Despite the long international flight, the illuminated billboards, crowds and scale of the place made for an exhilarating first day in the United States.

THE EVENING EXPLORATIONS

Over the following evenings, I explored the High Line, an elevated park built on a former railway line that offers a remarkable perspective on the city. SoHo brought a different atmosphere, with its cast-iron architecture, boutiques and streets made for wandering.

My exploration also took me into the heart of New York University. NYU's Washington Square campus is not enclosed within a traditional, self-contained campus; its academic buildings and student spaces are woven into the surrounding streets of Greenwich Village. Washington Square Park feels like the unofficial heart of the campus, shared by students, residents, street performers and visitors. I also came across Union Square Park — two lively public spaces where university life and everyday New York blend effortlessly.

I spent time around the World Trade Center too, taking in the scale of the site and its striking modern architecture. Each evening became its own small expedition: a subway ride, a neighbourhood to explore, a new place to eat and another side of the city to discover.

These evenings set the rhythm for the trip. I wasn't trying to see everything in one go. I was learning to navigate an unfamiliar country, getting comfortable with public transport and leaving room for spontaneous decisions.

New York was the prologue.
The road trip was the adventure that followed.

THE ROAD JOURNALDAY BY DAY

Eight days.
Eight different Americas.

Not every plan worked out. The rain changed a few stops, one show had already started, and some of the best discoveries were never on the itinerary.

01THE CROSSING
FRIDAY, SEPTEMBER 25NEW YORK · CONNECTICUT · RHODE ISLAND

Long Island, the ferry crossing
and Providence

Roslyn Harbour → Port Jefferson → Bridgeport → Providence area

We left home in Roslyn Harbour around 4:30 p.m., ready to begin the road trip. Rather than drive around the coast, we headed to Port Jefferson for the 6:45 p.m. ferry to Bridgeport, Connecticut.

Before boarding, we stopped at Tommy's Place for drinks and sides. The crossing took approximately an hour and fifteen minutes — a welcome change from the usual highway grind. There was something satisfying about beginning a long American road trip on the water.

After arriving in Bridgeport, we continued through Connecticut's coastal corridor, passing Milford, New Haven and New London before making our way towards Rhode Island. Dinner was at Shell & Bones Oyster Bar and Grill around 9 p.m. We ended the evening at Holiday Inn Express near Smithfield, outside Providence.

THE DAY'S CHARACTER

A maritime beginning to an ambitious journey, with the first taste of New England's coastal geography.

02CITY & CAMPUS
SATURDAY, SEPTEMBER 26MASSACHUSETTS · NEW HAMPSHIRE · VERMONT

Boston, baseball
and Cambridge

Providence area → Boston → New Hampshire → Colchester

We set off for Boston around 11:40 a.m. and explored the harbour area before driving through downtown East Boston. We then made our way to Boston Common, where we visited the Soldiers and Sailors Monument.

The next stop was Fenway Park, one of America's most recognisable baseball stadiums. Even without attending a game, seeing a historic American sports venue added another dimension to the trip.

By mid-afternoon, we were in Harvard Square for lunch at Santouka, enjoying Japanese ramen at around 3:42 p.m. It was an interesting contrast: iconic American landmarks followed by a Japanese meal in one of the country's best-known university neighbourhoods.

Later, we continued north, stopping at a Mexican bar in New Hampshire before reaching Motel 6 in Colchester, Vermont, for the night.

THE DAY'S CHARACTER

Urban landmarks, American sports culture and university-town atmosphere — with Vermont waiting for the next morning.

03THE MOUNTAIN ROAD
SUNDAY, SEPTEMBER 27VERMONT · UPSTATE NEW YORK

Lake Champlain, Smugglers' Notch
and a race against closing time

Colchester → Burlington → Shelburne → Jeffersonville → Stowe → Lake George

This was one of the most scenic and spontaneous days of the trip. We drove into downtown Burlington, passing the University of Vermont, and continued to the waterfront overlooking Lake Champlain. At Spot on the Dock, we stopped for lunch and drinks, enjoying the lakeside setting. We also caught sight of a train arriving at Burlington station — a small moment that made the town feel especially alive.

Next came Shelburne Farms. Unfortunately, the rain meant we couldn't take the tour. Rather than let that derail the day, we continued towards Jeffersonville and stopped at El Gato Cantina around 5 p.m.

Then came the mountain drive through Smugglers' Notch and Stowe. The winding road through Vermont's mountain terrain was exactly the kind of experience that makes a road trip different from a conventional holiday.

We reached Ben & Jerry's Factory around 6:45 p.m., just fifteen minutes before closing. There was just enough time to enjoy some ice cream before setting off for Lake George, roughly two and a half hours away. We checked into Holiday Inn Express that night.

THE DAY'S CHARACTER

Rain changed one plan, but a mountain pass, a local stop and a last-minute ice-cream run made the day memorable anyway.

04A SLOWER MORNING
MONDAY, SEPTEMBER 28NEW YORK · PENNSYLVANIA

Lake George, café culture
and the road to Carlisle

Lake George → Carlisle, Pennsylvania

After the previous day's long drive, we started with a relaxed morning walking around Lake George. We visited the Steamboat Company store and then walked to Café Vero for a particularly enjoyable coffee and breakfast.

Café Vero was more than just a café. Discovering its miniature brewery was an unexpected bonus — one of those small details you rarely plan for but remember afterwards.

We also visited Fort William Henry, adding a little history to the lakeside morning. At around 2:30 p.m., we set off again, heading south and west through New York and Pennsylvania. Our overnight stop was Holiday Inn Express in Carlisle, Pennsylvania.

THE DAY'S CHARACTER

A welcome pause between Vermont's mountains and the historical destinations ahead.

05HISTORY & MEMORY
TUESDAY, SEPTEMBER 29PENNSYLVANIA · WASHINGTON · VIRGINIA

Gettysburg and the monuments
of Washington

Carlisle → Gettysburg → Washington, DC → Georgetown → Fairfax

We left Carlisle around 10:30 a.m. and headed to Gettysburg National Military Park. Touring the battlefield by car, we visited several memorials, including Little Round Top, one of the best-known locations associated with the Battle of Gettysburg.

The day then shifted from Civil War history to America's national story. We visited the Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum and saw several of Washington's iconic monuments, including the World War II Memorial, Washington Monument and Lincoln Memorial.

By evening, we were in Georgetown. We stopped at Sovereign and had dinner at Reren, a Chinese restaurant. On the way back towards our overnight area, we also visited George Mason University in Fairfax.

THE DAY'S CHARACTER

History at two scales — the battlefield that helped shape the nation and the monuments that commemorate its ideals.

06MUSIC COUNTRY
WEDNESDAY, SEPTEMBER 30VIRGINIA · TENNESSEE

Bristol, Knoxville
and Nashville after dark

Carlisle area → Bristol → Knoxville → Nashville

We left around 10:45 a.m. for Nashville, driving through the mountain region. In Bristol, we stopped at Legit's Kitchen for soul food before visiting the Birthplace of Country Music Museum, a fitting introduction to the musical heritage of the region.

We continued to Knoxville, where we took a coffee break at Awaken Coffee in the Old City and captured photographs at the Virginia–Tennessee border.

We finally reached Nashville at approximately 9:10 p.m. The timing was unfortunate: the show at the Bluebird Cafe had already started at 9 p.m., so we missed it.

But Nashville had plenty more to offer. We parked near Lower Broadway and walked through the neon-lit entertainment district, moving between live-music bars. Robert's Western World and Posty's became our main stops for food and the unmistakable energy of Nashville nightlife. We returned to Robert's before finally reaching our downtown hotel at 2:30 a.m.

THE DAY'S CHARACTER

Missing one show led to a spontaneous night of live music, Southern food and Nashville's after-dark atmosphere.

07MUSIC & CRAFT
THURSDAY, OCTOBER 1NASHVILLE · LYNCHBURG · MEMPHIS

The Ryman Auditorium,
Lynchburg and Memphis

Nashville → Lynchburg → Memphis

After the late night in Nashville, we left the hotel around 11 a.m. and visited Ryman Auditorium. We watched a show about the venue's history and picked up memorabilia — a chance to understand why this historic building occupies such an important place in American music.

We then drove to Lynchburg for the Jack Daniel's distillery tour, arriving around 1:45 p.m. The visit took approximately three and a half hours. We explored the distillery, browsed the gift shop and collected memorabilia, and saw Mason's Hall from outside.

This was an opportunity to experience a famous American brand in the small Tennessee town where its story is rooted. We then headed west for Memphis. After a long drive, we arrived at around 11 p.m.

THE DAY'S CHARACTER

A transition from Nashville's musical history to Tennessee's distilling heritage, with Memphis waiting at the end of the road.

08THE LONG WAY TO TEXAS
FRIDAY, OCTOBER 2 — ARRIVAL OCTOBER 3TENNESSEE · ARKANSAS · TEXAS

Elvis, the Mississippi River
and the final push to Houston

Memphis → North Little Rock → Little Rock → Texarkana → Houston

Our final day on this leg of the journey was also the most ambitious. We set off around 10:30 a.m. intending to visit Graceland, Elvis Presley's mansion. Heavy rain changed the plan. With the tour requiring two to three hours and the admission price feeling excessive for the time available, we decided to skip the mansion and instead browse the Elvis gift store for memorabilia.

We then visited Aretha Franklin's birthplace, walked along Beale Street and stopped at Charles Vergos' Rendezvous for its famous charcoal-grilled dry pork ribs.

After lunch, we headed to Mud Island River Park. On the way, we passed the striking Pyramid building, now home to Bass Pro Shops. At Mud Island, we found the iconic Memphis sign and walked along the Mississippi River trail.

The trail was a standout experience. Its carefully constructed representation of the Mississippi's twists and bends, with the real river flowing alongside, made the walk both educational and beautiful. It was a fitting way to experience one of America's great geographical landmarks.

We left Memphis around 3:30 p.m., initially intending to stop at Little Rock and Texarkana. Before reaching Little Rock, however, we discovered the Old Mill in North Little Rock. The picturesque water mill, surrounded by greenery and water, looked almost like a scene from a fairy tale. Even the drive back towards the highway, past beautiful houses, became part of the experience.

In Little Rock, we saw Junction Bridge and read about the city's history, then stopped for coffee at Starbucks at the Courtyard by Marriott before leaving around 7:15 p.m.

By approximately 9:30 p.m., we were in Texarkana, where we visited the Arkansas–Texas state line near the US Post Office. We had dinner at Verona, an Italian restaurant, before setting off again at 10:30 p.m. We finally reached Houston at around 3:30 a.m. on October 3.

THE DAY'S CHARACTER

Music, food, a great river, a fairy-tale mill, local history and a determined final push into Texas. The trip in miniature.

THE THINGS BETWEEN THE STOPS

The best discoveries
weren't always on the map.

Café Vero. A train arriving in Burlington. A last-minute scoop of ice cream. The Old Mill near Little Rock. The Mississippi River trail. The small, unplanned moments that turned a route into a story.

LOOKING BACKTHE TAKEAWAY

Several Americas.
One journey.

Looking back, what made this journey special was its variety.

We began with New England's coastal geography and Boston's urban landmarks. Vermont brought mountain roads, lakeside towns and scenic drives. Gettysburg and Washington offered a deeper connection to American history. Tennessee introduced us to country music, live-music culture and whiskey heritage. Memphis brought the legacy of Elvis Presley, Aretha Franklin and the blues, alongside the Mississippi River. Arkansas surprised us with the Old Mill and Little Rock's history. Finally, Texarkana marked our crossing into Texas and the end of the road trip in Houston.

But the destinations alone don't tell the whole story. Some of the most memorable experiences were the smaller discoveries: breakfast at Café Vero, the train arriving in Burlington, the last-minute stop at Ben & Jerry's, the live-music bars of Lower Broadway and the unexpected beauty of the Old Mill.

The trip wasn't perfectly planned, and not everything went according to schedule. Rain prevented our tour at Shelburne Farms. We missed the Bluebird Cafe show. We skipped Graceland's mansion tour. Yet these changes never defined the trip negatively. They simply created space for other experiences.

The final drive from Memphis to Houston was long, and arriving at 3:30 a.m. was exhausting. But it also gave the journey a fitting ending: eight days of moving through different landscapes, histories and cultures, with the road itself connecting them all.

We didn't just drive between famous tourist destinations.
We discovered America one road, one town, one meal and one unexpected stop at a time.
