The Ultimate
American Road Trip

Long Island, New York, to Houston, Texas

Eight days. Multiple states. A ferry across Long Island Sound, Vermont mountain passes, Civil War battlefields, country music, a legendary whiskey distillery, the musical heritage of Memphis, and the mighty Mississippi River — followed by a late-night drive across Arkansas and Texas.

What made this journey memorable was not simply a tour of famous attractions. It was shaped by spontaneous decisions, local food, unexpected discoveries, and the freedom to stop when something caught your attention.

You began in Long Island with a ferry crossing and New England ahead of you. Eight days later, after travelling through the American South and across Arkansas, you arrived in Houston at 3:30 a.m.

The defining theme: discovering America one road, one town, one meal and one unexpected stop at a time.

BEFORE THE ROAD TRIP

A few days in New York

I arrived in New York on September 19 and stayed at the Hilton Garden Inn on Whitehall Street, close to South Ferry. Before setting off on the road trip, I spent the evenings getting to know the city.

I took the Staten Island Ferry and saw the Statue of Liberty, had lunch at River Dock Cafe, and took my first subway ride to Times Square. Over the next few days I explored the High Line, SoHo and the World Trade Center area.

I also walked through NYU's Washington Square campus and Washington Square Park, where university life spills into Greenwich Village, and visited Union Square Park. These walks were a good introduction to navigating an unfamiliar city before the longer drive began.

THE TRAVEL JOURNAL

Day by day

DAY 1 · FRIDAY, SEPTEMBER 25 · NEW YORK, CONNECTICUT AND RHODE ISLAND

The journey begins: Long Island to Providence

Roslyn Harbour → Port Jefferson → Bridgeport → Providence area

We left home in Roslyn Harbour at around 4:30 p.m., ready to begin the road trip. Rather than drive through New York and around the coast, we headed to Port Jefferson for the 6:45 p.m. ferry to Bridgeport, Connecticut.

Before boarding, we stopped at Tommy's Place for drinks and sides. The ferry crossing took approximately an hour and fifteen minutes — a welcome change from the usual highway grind. There was something satisfying about beginning a long American road trip on the water.

We reached Connecticut and continued towards New Haven, passing through the coastal corridor before dinner at Shell & Bones Oyster Bar and Grill around 9 p.m. By the end of the evening, we had made our way to the Providence area for an overnight stay at Holiday Inn Express near Smithfield.

Day in review: A maritime beginning to an ambitious journey, with the first taste of New England's coastal geography.

DAY 2 · SATURDAY, SEPTEMBER 26 · MASSACHUSETTS AND VERMONT-BOUND

Boston, baseball and a taste of Cambridge

Providence area → Boston → New Hampshire → Colchester, Vermont

We set off for Boston around 11:40 a.m. and explored the harbour area before driving through downtown East Boston. We then made our way to Boston Common, where we visited the Soldiers and Sailors Monument.

The next stop was Fenway Park, one of America's most recognisable baseball stadiums. Even without attending a game, seeing a historic American sports venue added another dimension to the trip.

By mid-afternoon, we were in Harvard Square for lunch at Santouka, enjoying Japanese ramen — an interesting contrast to the American landmarks and university neighbourhood around us.

Later, we continued north, stopping for drinks at a Mexican bar in New Hampshire before reaching Motel 6 in Colchester, Vermont, for the night.

Day in review: Urban exploration, American sports culture and university-town atmosphere — with Vermont waiting for the next morning.

DAY 3 · SUNDAY, SEPTEMBER 27 · VERMONT TO UPSTATE NEW YORK

Vermont's lakes, mountain passes and a race against closing time

Colchester → Burlington → Shelburne → Jeffersonville → Smugglers' Notch → Stowe → Lake George

This was one of the most scenic and spontaneous days of the trip.

We drove into downtown Burlington, passing the University of Vermont, and continued to the waterfront overlooking Lake Champlain. At Spot on the Dock, we stopped for lunch and drinks. We also saw a train arriving at Burlington station — a small moment that made the town feel especially alive.

Rain meant we couldn't take the planned tour at Shelburne Farms. Instead, we continued towards Jeffersonville and stopped at El Gato Cantina for drinks around 5 p.m.

Then came the mountain drive through Smugglers' Notch and Stowe. We reached Ben & Jerry's Factory around 6:45 p.m., just fifteen minutes before closing, with enough time for ice cream before driving roughly two and a half hours to Lake George.

We checked into Holiday Inn Express in Lake George that night.

Day in review: Rain changed one plan, but a mountain pass, a local bar and a last-minute ice-cream stop made the day memorable.

DAY 4 · MONDAY, SEPTEMBER 28 · NEW YORK TO PENNSYLVANIA

Lake George, café culture and the road to Carlisle

Lake George → Carlisle, Pennsylvania

We started with a relaxed morning walking around Lake George. We visited the Steamboat Company store and then walked to Café Vero for coffee and breakfast.

Discovering Café Vero's miniature brewery was an unexpected bonus — one of those small details you rarely plan for but remember afterwards.

We also visited Fort William Henry. At around 2:30 p.m., we set off south and west through New York and Pennsylvania, spending the night at Holiday Inn Express in Carlisle.

Day in review: A slower travel day and a welcome pause between Vermont's mountains and the historical destinations ahead.

DAY 5 · TUESDAY, SEPTEMBER 29 · PENNSYLVANIA, WASHINGTON, DC AND VIRGINIA

From the Civil War battlefield to the monuments of Washington

Carlisle → Gettysburg → Washington, DC → Georgetown → Fairfax

We left Carlisle around 10:30 a.m. and headed to Gettysburg National Military Park. Touring the battlefield by car, we visited several memorials, including Little Round Top.

The day then shifted from Civil War history to America's national story. We visited the Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum and saw the World War II Memorial, Washington Monument and Lincoln Memorial.

By evening, we were in Georgetown. We stopped for drinks at Sovereign and had dinner at Reren, a Chinese restaurant. On the way back towards our overnight area, we also visited George Mason University in Fairfax.

Day in review: History at two scales — the battlefield that helped shape the United States and the national institutions that represent it today.

DAY 6 · WEDNESDAY, SEPTEMBER 30 · VIRGINIA AND TENNESSEE

Across the mountains to the home of country music

Fairfax area → Bristol → Knoxville → Nashville

We left around 10:45 a.m. for Nashville, driving through the mountain region. In Bristol, we stopped at Legit's Kitchen for soul food before visiting the Birthplace of Country Music Museum.

We continued to Knoxville, took a coffee break at Awaken Coffee in the Old City and captured photographs at the Virginia–Tennessee border.

We reached Nashville around 9:10 p.m., too late for the Bluebird Cafe show that had started at 9. Instead, we parked near Lower Broadway and explored the neon-lit entertainment district. Robert's Western World and Posty's became our main stops for food, drinks and live music. We returned to Robert's before reaching our downtown hotel at 2:30 a.m.

Day in review: A long mountain drive that ended in a night of live music, Southern food and Nashville's after-dark atmosphere.

DAY 7 · THURSDAY, OCTOBER 1 · NASHVILLE, LYNCHBURG AND MEMPHIS

The Ryman Auditorium, Jack Daniel's and a late arrival in Memphis

Nashville → Lynchburg → Memphis

After the late night in Nashville, we left the hotel around 11 a.m. and visited Ryman Auditorium. We watched a show about the venue's history and picked up memorabilia.

We drove to Lynchburg for the Jack Daniel's distillery tour, arriving around 1:45 p.m. The visit took approximately three and a half hours. We explored the distillery, browsed the gift shop and saw Mason's Hall from outside.

This was more than a whiskey stop: it was a chance to experience a famous American brand in the small Tennessee town where its story is rooted. We then headed west and arrived in Memphis around 11 p.m.

Day in review: A transition from country music to whiskey heritage, followed by the anticipation of exploring Memphis.

DAY 8 · FRIDAY, OCTOBER 2 · MEMPHIS, ARKANSAS, TEXARKANA AND HOUSTON

Elvis, the Mississippi River and an unexpected journey across Arkansas

Memphis → North Little Rock → Little Rock → Texarkana → Houston

We set off around 10:30 a.m. intending to visit Graceland, Elvis Presley's mansion. Heavy rain changed the plan. With the tour requiring two to three hours and the admission price feeling excessive for the time available, we skipped the mansion and browsed the Elvis gift store for memorabilia.

We visited Aretha Franklin's birthplace, walked along Beale Street and stopped at Charles Vergos' Rendezvous for its famous charcoal-grilled dry pork ribs. After lunch, we headed to Mud Island River Park, passing the Pyramid building, now home to Bass Pro Shops.

At Mud Island, we found the iconic Memphis sign and walked along the Mississippi River trail. Its carefully constructed representation of the river's twists and bends, with the real river flowing alongside, made the walk both educational and beautiful.

We left Memphis around 3:30 p.m. Before reaching Little Rock, we discovered the Old Mill in North Little Rock. The picturesque water mill, surrounded by greenery and water, looked almost like a scene from a fairy tale.

In Little Rock, we saw Junction Bridge and read about the city's history, then stopped for coffee at Starbucks at the Courtyard by Marriott before leaving around 7:15 p.m.

By approximately 9:30 p.m., we were in Texarkana, where we visited the Arkansas–Texas state line near the US Post Office. We had dinner at Verona, an Italian restaurant, before setting off again at 10:30 p.m. We reached Houston at around 3:30 a.m. on October 3.

Day in review: The trip in miniature — music, food, a great river, an unexpected discovery, local history and a determined final push across state lines to Texas.

THE ROAD TRIP, IN RETROSPECT

One road, one town,
one unexpected stop at a time.

What made this journey memorable was not just the famous attractions. It was the ferry across Long Island Sound, Vermont's mountain passes, Civil War history, music in Nashville and Memphis, local meals, and the discoveries that weren't on the itinerary.

Rain changed plans at Shelburne Farms. We missed the Bluebird Cafe show and skipped the Graceland mansion tour. In their place came a train arriving in Burlington, ice cream just before closing, live music on Lower Broadway and the Old Mill in North Little Rock.

After leaving Memphis, we crossed Arkansas and Texarkana and arrived in Houston at 3:30 a.m. The road connected very different parts of America — and the journey was as much about the places between destinations as the destinations themselves.
